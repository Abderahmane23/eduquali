# Copywriting

## How to use this skill

1. **Gather the data** (§1). If the user hasn't provided it, ask for what's missing or state the assumptions.
2. **Set the message hierarchy** (§2): one promise, ordered beliefs, awareness stage.
3. **Choose a formula** (§4) matching goal, channel and audience temperature.
4. **Write the blocks** (§5–§6) so every sentence passes the three laws (§3) and the vocabulary rules (§7).
5. **Hold one voice** (§9) and apply the language rules (§10).
6. **Cut and review** with the checklist (§11), then **test** (§12).

Every rule below has a *weak → strong* example. Weak versions are typical first drafts; strong versions are what ships. Running example: a stock-management app for shops in Conakry.

---

## 1. Data to gather before writing

### 1.1 Audience — the "Who"
- **Pain points** — what frustrates them daily in this area.
- **Desired outcome** — what the day looks like once it's solved.
- **Objections** — price, time, complexity, trust: which one is loudest.
- **Their language** — exact phrases they use when complaining or praising. Collect from reviews (yours and competitors', especially 1★ and 3★), support and WhatsApp chats, sales calls, app-store reviews, surveys.
- **Awareness stage** — how much they already know (see §2.3).

Build a **swipe sheet** with five columns: memorable phrases · what they want · what makes them angry · positive emotions · negative emotions. Each column maps to a copy slot (headline, promise, agitation, testimonial framing, ad hook). The most frequent phrase, in their words, is the headline candidate.

> *A shop owner reviewing a stock app:* "I lose an hour every evening counting what's left." → headline slot: **"Know your stock without counting it."**

### 1.2 Product & offer — the "What"
- **Features → benefits**: what it does, then what that changes for the reader.
- **The offer**: exactly what they get (trial, discount, bonus, guarantee, delivery).
- **The mechanism**: how it works and why it succeeds where alternatives failed.

> *Weak:* "Real-time inventory sync." → *Strong:* "Every sale updates your stock the second it's paid — no evening counts."

### 1.3 Market — the "Context"
- Competitor weaknesses (from their reviews).
- The one thing only this product can claim (audience, mechanism, number, place).

### 1.4 Proof — the "Trust"
- Testimonials with names and results, case studies, metrics, ratings, logos.
- Authority: licences, regulators, certifications, awards, years, press.
- Brand voice guidelines if they exist.

## 2. Message hierarchy

### 2.1 One promise
Write a single sentence before anything else:
**"<Reader> gets <outcome> without <pain> because <mechanism>."**

> "Boutique owners get their stock right every night without counting, because every sale updates it automatically."

One page = one promise = one primary CTA.

### 2.2 Order of beliefs
Order the page by what the reader must believe to act, not by what the company wants to say:
**outcome → why it's credible → how it works → what it costs → what happens next.**
Every block answers "so what?" for the reader; a block that only answers "what is it?" is rewritten.

> *Weak block title:* "Our features" → *Strong:* "What changes on day one"

### 2.3 Awareness stage → opening

| Stage | Reader knows… | Open with | Example opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | nothing | a concrete story or observation | "Every evening at 19 h, Mariama counts 340 items by hand." |
| Problem-aware | the pain | the pain in their words (PAS) | "Tired of stock counts that never match the cash?" |
| Solution-aware | the category | why this approach, then proof | "Stock apps fail offline. Ours works without network." |
| Product-aware | your product | offer, proof, guarantee | "Free for 30 days. 120 shops in Conakry already use it." |
| Most aware | ready to buy | price and CTA only | "25 000 GNF/month. Start today." |

Ad, email and search traffic land at different stages; one page per stage when volume justifies it. PAS on a cold audience reads as pushy; AIDA on a warm audience reads as slow.

## 3. The three laws — every sentence passes all three

| Law | Test | Weak → Strong |
|---|---|---|
| **Concrete** | Can the reader picture it? | "Great support" → "A human answers in under 10 minutes." |
| **Falsifiable** | Can it be proven true or false? | "Fast delivery" → "Delivered in 30 minutes or it's free." |
| **Only you could sign it** | Could a competitor paste this sentence? | "The best truck on the market" → "Tougher than an F-150, faster than a Porsche." |

Companion rules:
- A great sentence is a good sentence made shorter. Delete until removing one more word breaks the meaning.
- Any word not working for the reader works against you: adverbs, intensifiers, hedges go first.
  > "We truly believe our app can really help you save time." → "Save an hour a day."
- Make a promise in the title; deliver it in the body.
- A sales page makes one bold claim at the top and spends the rest proving it.
- Copy reads like the customer wrote it. Read it aloud; if you wouldn't say it to them, rewrite it.
- Write in the design (Figma, page builder), never in a blank document: line breaks and hierarchy are part of the message.

## 4. Formulas — by goal, channel and temperature

| Formula | Steps | Best for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AIDA** | Attention → Interest → Desire → Action | cold audience, long pages, launches, email sequences | "Your stock, always right." → "Most shops lose 1 h/day counting." → "Sales update stock automatically; 120 shops already switched." → "Start free" |
| **PAS** | Problem → Agitate → Solution | problem-aware readers; ads, hero, cold email, WhatsApp (< 300 words) | "Counting stock every night?" → "That's 30 hours a month, and the count still doesn't match the cash." → "Every sale updates your stock the second it's paid." |
| **BAB** | Before → After → Bridge | transformation offers (fitness, finance, learning) | "Tonight: 340 items, a notebook, 19 h to 20 h." → "Tonight: home at 19 h, stock already correct." → "One app, works offline." |
| **FAB** | Feature → Advantage → Benefit | feature-heavy products (SaaS, banking apps); always end on the benefit | "Offline mode → keeps selling when the network drops → no lost sales on market day." |
| **IDCA** | Interest → Desire → Conviction → Action | already-interested readers; heavy proof before the ask | skip the hook; open with the outcome, stack testimonials and numbers, then CTA |
| **Star–Story–Solution** | a person → what happened → how it resolved | non-profit, luxury, founders, social posts | "Mariama, Madina market. Lost 2 M GNF in a year to miscounts. Since March: zero." |
| **Hero's Journey** | goal → obstacles → product as guide → success | long-form stories, case studies, video scripts | the customer is the hero; the product is the guide, never the hero |
| **4 U's** | Useful · Urgent · Unique · Ultra-specific | headlines, subject lines — score 0–4, ship ≥ 3 | "Stop counting stock: 120 Conakry shops did this month" (4/4) |
| **4 C's** | Clear · Concise · Compelling · Credible | quality gate for any short copy or social post | apply as a check, not a structure |

Sequences: alternate PAS → BAB → AIDA across a retargeting or email series; never the same formula three times in a row.

## 5. The four qualities every piece must show

| Quality | Rule | Weak → Strong |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear & simple** | Reader-focused, short sentences, plain words, skimmable in seconds | "Our platform leverages synergies to optimise your workflow." → "Your stock updates itself." |
| **Emotional connection** | Names the pain, shows the benefit, paints the after-state | "Inventory management software." → "Go home at 19 h with the stock already right." |
| **Trust & authority** | Real numbers and proof instead of hype; confident, not pushy | "The best app in Guinea." → "Used in 120 shops in Conakry. Rated 4.8★." |
| **Powerful CTA** | One next step, exact and low-risk | "Submit" → "Start my free month — no card needed" |

## 6. Writing the blocks

### 6.1 Landing / product page

| Block | Rule | Weak → Strong |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline (H1)** | ≤ 10 words; outcome or strongest quantified claim; sentence case; no exclamation mark; no question the reader can answer "no" to | "Welcome to StockPro!" → "Your stock, right every night, without counting." |
| **Sub-headline** | One sentence: who it's for + how it works | "For boutiques and depots in Guinea. Every sale updates stock automatically, even offline." |
| **Primary CTA** | Outcome verb, first person on commitment buttons; one reassurance micro-line under it | "Sign up" → "Start my free month" + "No card required" |
| **Proof line** | Directly under the hero; named + quantified; logos only as support | "Trusted by many" → "120 shops in Kaloum and Madina · 4.8★ on Play Store" |
| **How it works** | Three numbered steps, verb phrases ≤ 8 words; step 1 doable today | "1. Download the app. 2. Add your products. 3. Sell — stock updates itself." |
| **Feature/benefit blocks** | Header = benefit ≤ 6 words; body = one concrete sentence with the feature as proof; max 3 per row | "Offline mode" → **"Sell when the network drops"** — "Sales are saved on the phone and synced when you're back online." |
| **Testimonial** | One, with name, photo, role/city and a specific result; keep their phrasing | "Great app!" → "I stopped counting at night. Since March, zero difference between stock and cash." — Mariama D., boutique, Madina |
| **Objections / FAQ** | The five questions the sales team hears most, in customers' words; answer in ≤ 2 sentences, answer first | "Does it work without internet?" → "Yes. Sales are stored on the phone and sync later." |
| **Pricing** | Price first, then what's included, then guarantee; savings in money, not % | "Save 20 %!" → "25 000 GNF/month. Pay yearly and save 60 000 GNF." |
| **Final CTA** | Restate the promise in one line + primary CTA + micro-line | "Ready to get started?" → "Go home with the stock already right. Start my free month." |

### 6.2 App-store / marketplace listing
- First line = the promise (it's all that shows before "more").
- 3–5 benefit bullets starting with verbs.
- Screenshot captions state outcomes, not feature names.
  > "Dashboard screen" → "See today's sales and stock in one glance"

### 6.3 Social post
- First line is the hook (a number, a contrast or the pain). One idea. One CTA or none.
  > "Excited to announce our new update 🎉" → "120 shops stopped counting stock by hand this month. Here's what they do instead:"

## 7. Vocabulary

**Use** — words that shift focus to the reader and lower friction:

| Word | Why | Example |
|---|---|---|
| you / your (vous / votre) | reader-centred | "Your stock" not "Our inventory module" |
| because (parce que) | gives the reason | "Start today, because tonight's count is 60 minutes you won't get back." |
| get / find / discover (obtenez / trouvez) | active, immediate value | "Get your evening back" |
| free / simple / easy (gratuit / simple) | lowers perceived effort or risk — only when true | "Free for 30 days" |
| imagine (imaginez) | opens the after-state | "Imagine leaving at 19 h with the stock already right." |
| now / today (maintenant / aujourd'hui) | momentum without panic | "Start today" |
| fix / solve / stop (réglez / arrêtez) | targets the pain | "Stop counting stock at night" |

**Avoid** — and what to write instead:

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| synergy, paradigm shift, leverage, utilize | corporate fluff | plain verbs: use, work with |
| revolutionary, disruptive, innovative solution, révolutionnaire | overclaims, triggers scepticism | the concrete mechanism or number |
| world-class, best-in-class, next-gen, leader, seamless | unprovable | a falsifiable claim |
| cheap, bargain | lowers perceived value | the price and what it includes |
| buy, purchase (on buttons) | reminds of losing money | "Get", "Start", "Order", "Book" |
| hurry, act fast, last chance (without a real deadline) | fake urgency breaks trust | the real deadline, or nothing |
| really, truly, genuinely, honestly, vraiment, réellement | filler intensifiers | delete |
| n'hésitez pas, don't hesitate | passive, dated | the direct action: "Écrivez-nous sur WhatsApp" |

## 8. Short-form: email, WhatsApp, SMS, ads, notifications

**Email**
- Subject ≤ 45 characters, ultra-specific; preheader completes it.
  > "Newsletter #12" → "Your March stock report is ready"
- First line is the point. One idea, one CTA; ≤ 150 words promotional, ≤ 300 story. Written to one person, signed by a real name.
- Transactional (order, payment, OTP): what happened · reference · what's next · one link. Nothing promotional.

**WhatsApp / SMS (agent or manual)**
- One message per reply, ≤ 3 lines, one question per message, no politeness-only messages.
- First message = who you are + why, in one line. Price, availability and next step in the same message when known.
  > "Bonjour ! Comment allez-vous ? 😊" → "Bonjour Mariama, Ibrahima de StockPro. Votre commande de 12 cartons est prête : 180 000 GNF, livrée demain avant 17 h. On confirme ?"
- Local number formats ("15 000 GNF"), delivery time as a fact, a reference number in every confirmation.

**Ads (Meta / Google)**
- Hook in the first line = the problem or the number; PAS condensed; one CTA matching the landing headline word for word.
- 3–5 variants per ad set, each a different **angle** (pain · outcome · proof · price · story), not five wordings of one angle.
  > Pain: "Still counting stock by hand?" · Outcome: "Home at 19 h, stock already right." · Proof: "120 Conakry shops switched in March." · Price: "25 000 GNF/month, first month free." · Story: "Mariama lost 2 M GNF to miscounts. Then…"

**Push / in-app**
- ≤ 60 characters, a reason to open, no marketing tone on transactional alerts; ≤ 1 promotional push per week.
  > "Check out what's new!" → "Low stock: 3 items below your minimum"

**Error / empty / success states** — follow design_skill.md §5 microcopy rules.
> "Error 42" → "Payment didn't go through. Check your Orange Money balance and try again."

## 9. Voice — pick one and hold it

Define the voice as **three adjectives + three "never" words** before drafting; every writer and every AI prompt gets that line. Personality strengthens the message or is removed. Humour only where the domain allows (restaurant/casual, D2C); never on payment, security, error or legal screens. Same term for the same thing everywhere.

**Styles analysed and when to borrow them**

| Style | Signature | Borrow for | Sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Apple** | short declarative fragments, concrete nouns, no adjectives, product as hero | premium products, luxury, hardware | "iCloud Drive. Any kind of file. On all your devices." |
| **Stripe** | precise, technical nouns; benefits framed as infrastructure; no hype | banks, fintech, B2B, APIs | "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue." |
| **Linear** | restraint; calm statements to experts; product shown working, not described | developer and professional tools | live demo in the hero instead of a claim |
| **Notion** | warm, human, playful short headlines, illustration-led | consumer productivity, education, community | "Meet the night shift." |
| **Basecamp** | blunt, opinionated, anti-complexity | challenger brands, SMB tools | "Stop running in circles and ship work that matters." |
| **Ahrefs** | opens with a specific dataset; teaches instead of sells | content marketing, B2B authority | "We analysed 920 million posts and found 90 % get no search traffic." |
| **Ramp** | volume-optimised low-friction CTAs, numbers everywhere | self-serve SaaS, fintech growth | "Get started free" |
| **Harry Dry** (Marketing Examples) | visual before/after rewrites, one idea per line, no fluff | newsletters, teaching content, LinkedIn | "Weak: Better service. Strong: Replies in 10 minutes." |
| **Joanna Wiebe** (Copyhackers) | customer's own words, researched long pages, "join the conversation in their head" | sales pages, onboarding emails, launches | headline lifted verbatim from a customer review |
| **Eddie Shleyner** (VeryGoodCopy) | micro-lessons, compression, story-first, one takeaway | newsletters, founder content | 150-word story, one lesson, one line |
| **Neville Medhora / Laura Belgray / Lianna Patch** | conversational, funny, personality-driven | restaurants, lifestyle, D2C, personal brands | "Poulet braisé so good your cousin will call." |
| **Dave Gerhardt** | founder voice, plain talk, short paragraphs, "write like you speak" | B2B social, launch emails | "We built this because we hated the old way. Here it is." |

**Voice per domain** (aligned with design_skill presets)

| Domain | Voice | Sample headline |
|---|---|---|
| Tech / professional / banks | Stripe + Linear: precise, quantified, calm, sentence case | "Transfers to 40 countries, settled in one day." |
| Restaurant / casual | Notion + Belgray: warm, verbs, appetite words, short | "Poulet braisé, prêt en 20 minutes. Commandez." |
| Non-profit | Wiebe + Ahrefs: human, "we", concrete outcomes, real numbers | "25 000 GNF = un mois d'eau potable pour un enfant." |
| Luxury / gallery | Apple: sparse fragments, uppercase labels, no exclamation, no urgency | "Pièces uniques. Édition de trois." |

## 10. French copy rules

- **Vous or tu**: choose once per brand, never mix. Vous for banks, professional, luxury, non-profit; tu only for casual consumer brands whose customers use it with each other.
- Write French natively; never translate English idioms.
  > "Get started" → "Commencer" (not "Démarrer maintenant !") · "You're all set!" → "C'est prêt."
- Typography: non-breaking space before `: ; ? ! %` and inside « guillemets »; thousands with a space; currency after the number ("15 000 GNF"); dates DD/MM/YYYY; hours "17 h" or "17h30".
- Sentence case for buttons and headings ("Créer mon compte"), never Title Case.
- Avoid anglicisms when a common French word exists (livraison, paiement, panier); keep the one customers actually use (WhatsApp, Mobile Money, QR code).
- UI strings ≤ 30 % longer than English; a label over two lines on a 360 px screen is rewritten.
  > "Procéder à la finalisation de votre commande" → "Valider ma commande"

## 11. Review checklist — run before shipping

1. Can the reader picture it? (concrete)
2. Could it be proven wrong? (falsifiable)
3. Could a competitor sign it? (delete if yes)
4. Is the promise in the first line?
5. Exactly one CTA, in the reader's words, with a reassurance line?
6. Every paragraph ≤ 2 lines on mobile?
7. All numbers real and sourced?
8. Every adverb, intensifier, hedge and banned word removed?
9. Does it read aloud like speech?
10. Same voice as the rest of the product?
11. FR/EN formats and vous/tu consistent?
12. Nothing left that can be removed without collapsing the meaning?

---

## 12. Test

- Test order: headline → hero proof → CTA label → sub-headline → offer framing → everything else.
- One variable per test; at least two full weeks; ship the winner and log both versions and the result in DECISIONS.md.
- Below testing volume: read the copy to five real customers, watch where they stop, frown or ask; rewrite those lines.
- Re-collect customer language every quarter; refresh the swipe sheet and the FAQ from it.
